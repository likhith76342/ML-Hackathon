# Candidate-miss investigation

These are development-data observations for discussion and for writing the team's own report; point references are approximate and are not independently verified ground truth.

Using the current 50 m radius and maximum of 10 candidates, 24,473 of 208,417 training observations (11.74%) do not contain their aligned reference arc. Of those misses, 23,255 have an aligned reference distance greater than 50 m and 1,218 are within 50 m. There are 18,229 missed points with no candidates; this count overlaps the distance categories. 20,538 training points have aligned reference distance above 100 m. Thus, increasing only the maximum candidate count cannot recover the outside-radius references. For within-radius misses, the candidate cap, distance ties or numerical boundary effects require further inspection. The student-test set has 52 misses out of 19,160 observations (0.27%).

The two training trips with the largest numbers of misses are 54 (17,512 misses) and 33 (3,923 misses). To investigate, we sampled every twentieth point in each trip and used exact STRtree nearest-segment queries. In trip 54, 85.7% of 966 sampled points were over 50 m from every network arc; the median nearest-road distance was 83.7 m. In trip 33, 0.7% of 293 sampled points were over 50 m from every road, but 59.0% were over 50 m from every reference-route arc. These checks show different spatial patterns; they do not establish whether GPS noise, the reference route, network coverage or another issue caused them.

Reproduce the aggregate counts with `python -m models.logreg.miss_diagnostics` and the targeted spatial spot-check with `python -m models.logreg.miss_spotcheck`. The scripts write `results/logreg/miss_diagnostics.json`, `miss_diagnostics_by_trip.csv`, and `miss_spotcheck.json`. The team should inspect maps and decide together before changing the radius, candidate count, labels, or shared dataset version; changing them would require rebuilding the dataset and retraining all models.
